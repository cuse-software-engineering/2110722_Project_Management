## Detailed answers

### Chapter 3 — The Project Management Process Groups

#### Q1

> The project management process groups are initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, reviewing, and closing.

**Answer: False.**

There are five process groups; the question adds a sixth, "reviewing", which does not exist. Everything that could be called "reviewing" (progress reports, sprint reviews) belongs to monitoring and controlling.

- Slide 3: "Project management process groups — Initiating processes; Planning processes; Executing processes; Monitoring and controlling processes; Closing processes"
- Slide 53: "The five project management process groups are initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, and closing"

Difference from the slide: the item "reviewing" is inserted.

#### Q2

> The initiating process group includes identifying a project to invest and authorizing the existence of a project.

**Answer: False.**

The slide defines the initiating process group as *defining and authorizing* a project or phase and *identifying stakeholders*. Deciding which project to invest in is strategic planning, which the lecture places under **pre-initiation**: "it is good practice to lay the groundwork for a project before it officially starts", with senior managers deciding which projects to pursue and the business case drafted before initiation. The question keeps the "authorizing" half of the slide bullet and replaces "identifying stakeholders" with "identifying a project to invest", which belongs to the pre-initiation / strategic-planning step, not to the initiating process group.

Item that differs: **"identifying a project to invest"** (question) in place of **"identifying stakeholders"** (slide 4); project selection is pre-initiation (slides 14–16).

- Ch3 slide 4: "Initiating processes — Defining and authorizing a project or project phase; Identifying stakeholders"
- Ch3 slide 14 (Pre-initiation): "Strategic planning (vision, mission, goals) should serve as the foundation for deciding which projects to pursue"
- Ch3 slide 15 (Pre-initiation): "It is good practice to lay the groundwork for a project before it officially starts — Senior managers often perform several pre-initiation tasks" (determine constraints, identify the sponsor, select the project manager, develop a business case …)
- Ch3 slide 17: "Initiating includes recognizing and starting a new project" — Table 3-3 lists only Develop project charter and Identify stakeholders as the initiating processes
- Ch4 slide 6: "The first step in project management is deciding what projects to do" — strategic planning, which precedes "1. Developing a Project Charter"

See the Confidence section: Schwalbe's own template table (attachment p21, Table 3-20) files the business-case and selection templates under Initiating, so a grader who treats selection as part of initiating would mark True; the lecture's explicit pre-initiation step makes False the better answer.

#### Q6

### Chapter 4 — Project Integration Management

#### Q15

> "A new government law requires an organization to report data in a new way." A project that addresses this issue is easier to get approved than the projects that address new opportunities.

**Answer: True.**

A new government law is a *directive* ("new requirements imposed by management, government, or external influence"). The textbook's point about this categorization is that projects addressing problems or directives are easier to get approved and funded than those addressing opportunities, because the organization must respond to them to avoid harming its business.

- Ch4 slide 12: "Categorization for selecting projects — Problems: Current or anticipated situations that prevent an organization from achieving its goals; Opportunities: Chances to improve the organization; Directives: New requirements imposed by management, government, or external influence"
- Textbook (Schwalbe, Ch. 4, "Categorizing IT projects"): "It is often easier to get approval and funding for projects that address problems or directives because the organization must respond to these categories to avoid hurting their business."

See the Confidence section: the slide transcript carries the definitions but not the "easier to get approved" sentence.

#### Q16

> NPK, ROI, payback, and IRR of potential projects can be calculated for project selection.

**Answer: True** (reading "NPK" as the typo for **NPV**).

The primary financial methods for project selection are NPV, ROI and payback analysis, and IRR is introduced on the ROI slide as another calculable measure. Note: "NPK" is not a project-management term (the original question file also says "NPK"); the question almost certainly means NPV. If an examiner read "NPK" literally the statement would be False, but it is marked True under the intended reading.

- Ch4 slide 10: "Methods for selecting projects — ... Performing net present value or other financial analyses"
- Ch4 slide 13: "Primary methods for determining the projected financial value of projects — Net present value (NPV) analysis; Return on investment (ROI); Break-even point and payback analysis"
- Ch4 slide 16: "Also, Internal Rate of Return (IRR) can be calculated by finding the discount rate that makes the NPV equal to zero — The higher the IRR, the better"

#### Q17

> Some projects may not create a project management plan even though it is used to coordinate all project planning documents.

**Answer: True.**

The slide states both halves: the plan is used to coordinate all project planning documents, and a small project would not need a separate one (charter, scope statement and Gantt chart may be enough).

- Ch4 slide 22: "A project management plan is used to coordinate all project planning documents and help guide a project's execution and control — Plans created in the other knowledge areas are subsidiary parts of the overall project management plan — A small project (e.g. involving a few people over a few months) would not need a separate project management plan (only project charter, scope statement, and Gantt chart might be needed)"

#### Q18

> An organization usually organizes mentorship, communities of practice, and workshops to transfer explicit knowledge of a project.

**Answer: False.**

Mentorships, communities of practice and workshops are the slide's examples of how *tacit* knowledge (beliefs, insight, experience) is shared through conversation and interaction. Explicit knowledge (documents and plans) is easy to store and distribute and does not need such mechanisms.

- Ch4 slide 27: "Explicit knowledge — Easily explained using words, pictures, or numbers and easy to communicate, store, and distribute, e.g. project documents and plans"; "Tacit knowledge — Difficult to express and highly personal, e.g. belief, insight, experience — Often shared through conversations and interactions between people, e.g. mentorships, communities of practice, workshops"

Difference from the slide: "tacit" became "explicit".

#### Q19

> In change control, a change request needs approval by a change control board, any changes that are made are recorded in a version control system and validated to check integrity with the requirements, and changes are also communicated to stakeholders.

**Answer: True.**

Each clause matches one component of the change control system: the CCB approves or rejects change requests; configuration management controls changes with version control and maintains integrity by auditing the configuration against the requirements; and a process for communicating changes keeps everyone informed.

- Ch4 slide 31: "A change control system includes — Change control board; Configuration management; Process for communicating changes"
- Ch4 slide 32: "Change control board (CCB) is a formal group of people responsible for approving or rejecting changes on a project"
- Ch4 slide 33: "Controlling changes to this configuration — Maintaining the traceability of this configuration, having a historical records of old versions to trace changes throughout the project (version control); Maintaining the integrity of this configuration — Auditing the product configuration to verify conformance to requirements"
- Ch4 slide 34: "Process for communicating changes is important to have everyone coordinated and informed about latest project information — It involves — Timely notification of change to everyone affected ..."
- Ch4 slide 29: "Communicate changes to top management and key stakeholders"

#### Q20

> For a project team, the most important output of closing the project is making a final report and marking all project documents as final versions.

**Answer: False.**

Closing is about finalizing all activities and transferring the completed work: the slides describe it as gaining stakeholder and customer acceptance of the final product, and list four main outputs, of which the final product/service/result transition (with the acceptance statement) and the organizational-process-asset updates (lessons learned) carry the project's value. The final report and marking documents as final are only two of these outputs and are never called the most important; the question promotes the paperwork over delivering and getting acceptance for the product.

- Ch4 slide 35: "To close a project or phase, you must finalize all activities and transfer the completed or cancelled work to the appropriate people — Main outputs — Project documents updates – marked as final version; Final product, service, or result, including Transition plan, Written statement that the terms of contract were met or some type of project completion form; Final report and presentation; Organizational process assets updates, including Project documentation, Project closure document, Historical information produced by the project in a useful format, Lessons-learned report, Post-implementation review information"
- Ch3 slide 31: "Involves gaining stakeholder and customer acceptance of the final products and services — Even if projects are not completed, they should be closed out to learn from the past"
- Ch3 slide 32: "Deliver final product, service, or result; Plan and execute transition to normal operation; ... Prepare lessons-learned report; ... Prepare final report, final presentation; Have project sponsor sign a client acceptance form"

Difference from the slide: the slide lists four main outputs with no ranking; the question picks the two documentary ones and calls them "the most important".
