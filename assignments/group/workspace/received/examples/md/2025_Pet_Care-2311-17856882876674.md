## Executive Summary

PetCare Co., Ltd., founded in 2025 in Bangkok, is a PetTech startup dedicated to transforming pet healthcare and lifestyle management in Thailand. With the country's pet market expanding at a CAGR of nearly 9% through 2032, driven by rising numbers of "pet parents," there is an urgent need for integrated, intelligent digital solutions that go beyond fragmented record-keeping and manual care practices.

The PetCare Application is proposed as a unified mobile platform that consolidates health records, care reminders, safety tracking, nutrition guidance, and digital certification into one secure, AI-powered ecosystem. It addresses six core pain points faced by pet owners to day Health Records, Care Routines, Pet Safety, Nutrition, Wellness Tracking

The project runs from 1 September 2025 to 13 November 2026 (≈ 14.5 months for core delivery). nder an Agile Scrum methodology with 4-week sprints, structured into three delivery phases:

- **Phase 1 – Core Foundation (Sep 2025 – Feb 2026):** Focus on building core functions such as digital health records, reminders, and pet profiles to attract and onboard pet owners.
- **Phase 2 – Partnership & Ecosystem Expansion (Mar – Jul 2026):** Strengthen platform credibility through collaborations with clinics, vets, and pet product partners.
- **Phase 3 – Global Expansion & Advanced Intelligence (Aug – Nov 2026):** Scale the platform internationally and integrate advanced AI features for predictive wellness and community engagement.

The development team is organized into four groups: Management/Product, Development & Innovation, Marketing & Customer Service, and Operations.

## 1 Business Case with financial analysis and exhibit

### 1.1 Introduction and Background

*[Figure 1: PetCare Co., Ltd., commercialize trademark — round brown logo with a cartoon dog, cat and rabbit, text "PetCare – ESTABLISHED IN 2025"]*

PetCare Co., Ltd., established in 2025 and headquartered in Bangkok, Thailand, is a PetTech startup dedicated to developing a mobile application that enhances pet healthcare and lifestyle management through innovation. Its flagship product, the Digital PetCare Assistant, empowers pet owners to manage health records, receive reminders for essential activities, track pet safety with IoT-enabled devices such as smart collars and GPS trackers, and access AI-driven nutritional and health insights. The application also generates digital health certificates that can be used as trusted references for breeding, trading, or consultations.

**Our Vision** to be Thailand's leading innovator in AI-driven digital pet care, enhancing the wellbeing of pets and their families while setting new standards for healthy, safe, and comprehensive pet management that honors pets as cherished family members.

**Our Mission** is to create accessible and reliable mobile solutions that leverage artificial intelligence to support pet owners in managing health and daily care with confidence. By offering proactive recommendations, predictive reminders, and personalized insights, PetCare seeks to transform pet care into a data-driven and preventive practice that enhances the overall well-being of pets.

### 1.2 Project Objective

1. Launch an integrated mobile application that centralizes pet health records, reminders, and safety tracking.
2. Provide nutrition logging and guidance with curated rules and human-reviewed content.
3. Offer a wellness dashboard with actionable insights for owners and clinics.
4. Enable integrations via APIs for clinics and vetted partners (e.g., IoT devices, insurers).

### 1.3 Project Background and As-is System

#### 1.3.1. Project Background

Thailand's pet market is expanding rapidly—its pet food segment is projected to grow at ~8.8% CAGR (2026–2032), according to Verified Market Research. This growth is driven by the increasing number of "pet parents" who view their pets as family members and are willing to invest in their health and well-being. Despite this market growth, digital pet care solutions in Thailand remain underdeveloped and fragmented. Most existing tools provide only basic record-keeping or single-purpose features, leaving pet owners without a comprehensive, intelligent system for proactive care.

The responsibility of pet ownership extends beyond companionship to encompass careful planning and continuous care. This project is grounded in the recognition that pet owners universally pursue five key objectives for their animals' well-being

- Love: Promoting physical activity, emotional well-being, and a sense of contentment.
- Safety: Ensuring the security, traceability, and physical protection of pets within their environment.
- Food: Providing high-quality, growth-supporting, and long-term sustenance.
- Health: Maintaining a regimen of preventive care, including vaccinations, routine medical examinations, and timely treatment of ailments.
- Intelligent Support: Utilizing contemporary tools, such as data-driven recommendation systems and artificial intelligence, to deliver personalized insights and proactive guidance.

*[Figure 2: Four pillars of pet care with intelligent support — illustration of a dog with the words LOVE, SAFETY, FOOD, HEALTH under the caption "WITH INTELLIGENT SUPPORT"]*

<!-- Page 11 -->

Pet care remains challenging due to the fragmented nature of relevant information. Critical data is often dispersed across disparate formats and locations, including paper records for medical history, digital calendars for reminders, product labels for nutritional information, and independent smart devices for activity monitoring. This lack of data integration and centralization creates significant friction, hindering owners' ability to efficiently and holistically manage their pets' care.

The proposed application is designed to directly address this critical gap. By centralizing disparate pet-related data and converting it into actionable insights, the system will not only empower owners to meet the fundamental goals of pet care but also generate valuable aggregated data. This data can, in turn, be leveraged to develop innovation across the pet care ecosystem, enabling stakeholders such as pet food manufacturers, veterinary clinics, and insurance providers to develop more effective, needs-based products and services. The platform's business model will be built upon this aggregated, anonymized data, which will be licensed to industry partners to inform product development, marketing strategies, and service improvements.

#### 1.3.2. As-is System

Currently, pet care activities are managed through fragmented tools and manual processes. Pet owners rely on paper records, separate mobile applications, and ad-hoc information sources. The following overview describes the As-is System, highlighting the key actors, processes, and limitations of the current practice.

##### 1.3.2.1. Actors

1. **Owner (Pet Parent)**
   Responsible for managing pet health, care routines, and daily activities using fragmented tools.
2. **Veterinary Clinic**
   Provides medical services, records vaccinations, and issues health certificates.
3. **Third-party Applications/Devices**
   GPS trackers, smart collars, and online resources used separately without integration.

##### 1.3.2.2. Current Processes

1. **Health Records**
   Pet health information is maintained in paper vaccination booklets.
   These booklets are easily lost or forgotten, and veterinarians rely on them during visits.
2. **Care Routines**
   Owners rely on memory or mobile phone calendars for reminders of vaccinations, deworming, or grooming.

<!-- Page 12 -->

   There is no centralized or reliable reminder system linked to actual health data.
3. **Pet Safety and Tracking**
   If GPS trackers or smart collars are used, owners must open brand-specific applications.
   Location data is not integrated with other health or care information.
4. **Nutrition Decisions**
   Owners often make decisions about pet food based on advertisements or general online sources.
   There is no personalized nutritional analysis tailored to the pet's breed, age, or health conditions.
5. **Wellness Tracking**
   Owners may log weight or feeding data manually in notes, but the information is scattered and unstructured.
   No holistic dashboard exists to present overall pet wellness.
6. **Breeding and Sale Proof**
   Health certificates are issued on paper by veterinarians.
   Certificates are difficult to validate, can be misplaced, and are vulnerable to forgery or misuse.

##### 1.3.2.3. Weaknesses

1. **Fragmented** Data is scattered across paper, notes, and multiple apps.
2. **Reactive** Owners usually react after problems occur, rather than practicing preventive care.
3. **Lack of Standards** No unified platform integrates health data, reminders, IoT tracking, and nutrition.
4. **Privacy and Security Risks** Paper documents and ad-hoc sharing methods lack proper protection.
5. **High Effort** Owners must manually manage multiple tools and processes, which is inefficient and error prone.

### 1.6 Analysis of options and recommendations

*Table 5 Analysis of options*

| Options | Description |
|---|---|
| Inhouse Development | **Advantages:** Tight control of roadmap and IP; faster iterations; cultural alignment.<br>**Disadvantages:** Requires hiring across mobile, backend, AI, DevOps, security; higher fixed cost. |
| Outsource Development | **Advantages:** Rapid capacity; access to diverse expertise; predictable per-sprint cost.<br>**Disadvantages:** Less product ownership; knowledge leakage; vendor lock-in risk. |
| Hybrid Development | **Advantages:** Core leadership in-house (PO/PM/Tech Lead/UX), delivery pods from vendor(s); best balance of speed, cost, and IP.<br>**Disadvantages:** Requires strong governance (RACI, code ownership, CI/CD, security reviews). |

**Recommendation:** In-House Development with select SaaS add-ons

**Rationale:** Core development and operations remain in-house to maintain control over quality, CI/CD, and compliance. Security testing is outsourced to certified providers for periodic independent validation, while the OTP service is procured as SaaS to ensure secure and scalable user authentication.

## 10. Tool for quality control

To ensure effective quality control within the PetCare mobile application project, a **Fishbone Diagram** was utilized to analyze the root causes behind the issue: "**New feature testing is slow due to heavy manual QA effort**." This tool supports systematic problem analysis by visualizing the contributing factors that lead to the main problem, allowing the team to clearly identify improvement opportunities. The Fishbone Diagram used in this project is structured based on the 6M Framework:

- Manpower (People)
- Methods (Processes)
- Machines (Tools/Infrastructure
- Materials (Test Data/Resources)
- Measurements (Metrics)
- Mother-nature (External Constraints/Environment)

*[Figure 8: Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram. Problem (head): "New feature testing is slow due to heavy manual QA effort". Causes by category:*
- *Manpower: Only one QA responsible for all testing; Developers don't support testing; Limited domain knowledge increases analysis time.*
- *Methods: No standard test case format; No regression checklist.*
- *Machines: No test automation; No centralized test data management.*
- *Materials: Manual recreation of test data; No Exception scenario test data prepared in advance.*
- *Measurements: No test coverage metric; Defect leakage to later sprints is not tracked; No measurement of test effort/time.*
- *Mother-nature: Change requests often introduced mid-sprint; Tight release schedules.]*
