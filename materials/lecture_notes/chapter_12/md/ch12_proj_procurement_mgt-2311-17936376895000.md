## Slide 4: Importance of Project Procurement Management

- Procurement means acquiring goods and/or services from an outside source
  - Other terms include purchasing and outsourcing
  - PMI defines outside source as source outside the project team, i.e. the same organization can be a supplier to the project team
- Why outsource?
  - Access skills and technologies
  - Allow the client organization to focus on its core business
  - Reduce both fixed and recurrent costs
    - Labor costs of hiring, firing, reassigning people to projects, or paying their salaries when they are between projects
  - Provide flexibility
    - Extra staff during peak workloads
  - Increase accountability
    - Contract are legally binding and there is more accountability for delivering work as stated in the contract

## Slide 8: Types of Contracts (1 of 7)

- Fixed price or lump sum contracts : involve a fixed total price for a well-defined product or service
  - Firm-fixed-price (FFP)
  - Fixed-price with economic price adjustment (FP-EPA)
    - Include provision for pre-defined final adjustment to the contract price due to market conditions that are beyond the control of both the buyer and seller, e.g. changes in the cost of labor and material, general inflation, fluctuations in the currency market
    - Beneficial for long-term projects or those that span multiple years

## Slide 9: Types of Contracts (2 of 7)

- Fixed price or lump sum contracts (cont.)
  - Fixed-price incentive fee (FPIF)
    - Point of Total Assumption (PTA): the total cost above which the seller bears all the costs of a cost overrun. Any cost overrun above the PTA is not shared by the buyer and totally absorbed by the seller

*(Equation)*

*PTA = ((Ceiling Price − Target Price) / Buyer's Share Ratio) + Target Cost*

| Term | Description |
|---|---|
| Target Cost | The estimated cost of the project. It does not include seller's profit |
| Target Fee | Seller's Fee or Profit |
| Target Price | Total estimated price of the project (including seller's fee).<br>Target Price = Target Cost + Target Fee |
| Buyer's Share Ratio (BSR) | In case of cost overrun, the ratio of the cost that buyer will bear. |
| Ceiling Price | Maximum Price that the buyer is willing to pay - really the maximum, not even a penny more! It includes Target Price plus buyer's share of the cost overrun. |

## Slide 10: Types of Contracts (3 of 7)

- Fixed price or lump sum contracts (cont.)
  - Fixed-price incentive fee (FPIF) (cont.)
    - Example terms of the contract

*(Dark code-style box:)*

```
Target Cost: $60,000
Target Fee: $15,000
Target Price: $75,000
Ceiling Price: $80,000
Buyer-Seller Share Ratio: 60:40
```

*(Diagram: a vertical scale of cost levels with brackets marking the ranges between them)*

| Level | Amount |
|---|---|
| Ceiling | 80,000 |
| PTA | 68,333 |
| | 65,000 |
| Target Cost | 60,000 |

- Bracket from 65,000 up to 80,000 (Ceiling): Target fee
- Bracket from 60,000 (Target Cost) up to 68,333 (PTA): Cost overrun
  - Bracket from 65,000 up to 68,333: Seller’s share of cost overrun 40%
  - Bracket from 60,000 up to 65,000: Buyer’s share of cost overrun 60%

---

- PTA = ((80,000 - 75,000)/0.6) + 60,000 = (5,000/0.6) + 60,000 = 8,333 + 60,000 = $68,333
- Maximum cost overrun that buyer will share cost = 68,333 - 60,000 = $8,333
- Buyer’s share of cost overrun = 0.6*8,333 = $5,000
- Amount buyer pays at PTA = Target Price (or Target Cost + Target Fee) + Buyer’s share of cost overrun = 75,000 + 5,000 = $80,000 (= Ceiling Price)
- The amount seller received at PTA = the amount buyer pays = $80,000
- The amount seller spent at PTA (actual cost) = $68,333
- The amount seller makes at PTA = 80,000 - 68,333 = + $11,666 (profit)
- In this case, seller is still making some profit even when the actual cost reaches PTA

## Slide 11: Types of Contracts (4 of 7)

- Cost-reimbursable contracts
  - Seller is reimbursed the actual costs that incur in carrying out the works, plus an additional fee
  - Cost plus incentive fee (CPIF)
    - The buyer pays the seller for allowable costs (as defined in the contract) plus a predetermined fee and incentive bonus
    - Incentives are provided for completing the project ahead of schedule or for reducing contract costs
  - Cost plus fixed fee (CPFF)
    - The buyer pays the seller for allowable costs (as defined in the contract) plus a fixed fee usually based on a percentage of estimated costs
  - Cost plus award fee (CPAF)
    - The buyer pays the seller for allowable costs (as defined in the contract) plus an award fee based on buyer’s satisfaction
  - Cost plus percentage of costs (CPPC)
    - The buyer pays the seller for allowable costs (as defined in the contract) plus a predetermined percentage based on total costs
    - Least desirable type of contract because the seller has no incentive to decrease costs

## Slide 13: Types of Contracts (6 of 7)

- Time and material contracts
  - Hybrid of both fixed price and cost reimbursable contracts
  - Based on actual time spent (e.g. hourly or daily rates), and actual materials used (e.g. hardware, software licenses, equipment, etc.).
  - E.g. a fee of $80 per hour for services, plus a fixed price of $10,000 for providing a specific project material (or a ceiling of $10,000)
  - Might include “not-to-exceed” clause in a contract
  - Often used when total costs cannot be specified in a contract
  - Preferred by many contract programmers and consultants
- Unit price contracts
  - Require the buyer to pay the seller a predetermined amount per unit of service or material.
  - E.g. the value of contract is $2,500 for laying wire $25/m² for 100 m² or $10,000 for buying 10 units of $1,000 computer
  - Often involve volume discount

## Slide 15: Tools and Techniques for Planning Procurement Management (1 of 2)

- Make-or-buy analysis
  - Determine whether an organization should
    - Make or perform a particular product or service inside the organization vs. buy from someone else, or
    - Purchase vs. lease items for the project
    - E.g. Equipment has a purchase price of $12,000 and daily operating costs of $400 vs. lease for $800 per day
      - Determine when the purchase cost equals the lease cost:

        $800/day = $12,000 + $400/day
        $400/day = $12,000
        d = 30
      - If equipment is needed < 30 days, lease it
      - If equipment is needed > 30 days, purchase it

## Slide 19: Statement of Work (SOW) (Figure 12-3)

*(Template document on a shaded background)*

**Statement of Work (SOW)**

- I. **Scope of Work:** Describe the work to be done in detail. Specify the hardware and software involved and the exact nature of the work.
- II. **Location of Work:** Describe where the work must be performed. Specify the location of hardware and software and where the people must perform the work.
- III. **Period of Performance:** Specify when the work is expected to start and end, working hours, number of hours that can be billed per week, where the work must be performed, and related schedule information.
- IV. **Deliverables Schedule:** List specific deliverables, describe them in detail, and specify when they are due.
- V. **Applicable Standards:** Specify any company or industry-specific standards that are relevant to performing the work.
- VI. **Acceptance Criteria:** Describe how the buyer organization will determine if the work is acceptable.
- VII. **Special Requirements:** Specify any special requirements such as hardware or software certifications, minimum degree or experience level of personnel, travel requirements, and so on.

## Slide 20: Procurement or Bid Documents

  - A proposal is a document prepared by a seller when there are different approaches for meeting buyer needs
- Request for Quotes (RFQ)
  - Used to solicit quotes or bids from prospective suppliers
  - A bid, also called a tender or quote (short for quotation), is a document prepared by sellers providing pricing for standard items that have been clearly defined by the buyer

## Slide 21: Source Selection Criteria

- It’s important to prepare some form of evaluation criteria, preferably before issuing a formal RFP or RFQ
- Organizations use criteria to rate or score proposals, and they often assign a weight to each criterion to indicate its importance, e.g.
  - Technical approach 30%
  - Management approach 30%
  - Past performance 20%
  - Price 20%
- Some IT projects also require potential sellers to deliver a technical presentation as part of their proposal
  - The proposed project manager should lead the potential seller’s presentation team

## Slide 27: 3. Controlling Procurements (1 of 4)

- Ensures the seller’s performance meets contractual requirements
  - Contracts are legal relationships, so it is important that legal and contracting professionals be involved in writing and administering contracts
    - Project team members must be aware of potential legal problems they might cause by not understanding a contract
- It is critical that project managers and team members watch for constructive change orders
  - A situation when a contractor performs work beyond the contract requirements, without a formal order under the changes clause, either due to an informal order from, or through the fault of, the buyer
  - Contractor may be entitled to payment for additional costs incurred, and an extension to the contract performance period.
  - This is also a change where the buyer and the seller cannot reach an agreement and could result in claims/disputes

## Slide 28: Controlling Procurements (2 of 4)

- Suggestions for change control in contracts
  - Use tools and techniques, such as a contract change control system, buyer-conducted performance reviews, inspections and audits, etc.
  - Changes to any part of the project need to be reviewed, approved, and documented by the same people in the same way that the original part of the plan was approved
  - Evaluation of any change should include an impact analysis
  - Changes must be documented in writing
  - Project managers and teams should stay closely involved to make sure the new system will meet business needs and work in an operational environment
  - Have backup plans in case the new system does not work as planned
