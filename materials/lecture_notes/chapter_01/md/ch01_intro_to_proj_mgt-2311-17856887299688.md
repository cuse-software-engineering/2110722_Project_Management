## Slide 13: What Is a Project?

- A project is "a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result" (PMBOK® Guide, Sixth Edition, 2017)
- An operation is work done to sustain the business, focusing on ongoing production of goods and services
- Projects end when their objectives have been reached or the project has been terminated
- IT project involves using hardware, software, and networks to create a product, service, or result.

## Slide 29: Program Management

- A program is "a group of related projects, subsidiary programs, and program activities managed in a coordinated manner to obtain benefits and control not available from managing them individually" (PMBOK® Guide — Sixth Edition, 2017)
  - Grouping is often more economical as it helps streamline management, staffing, purchasing, and other work.
- Examples of common programs in the IT field include
  - Infrastructure: provide more wireless access, upgrade h/w and s/w, enhance security.
  - Applications development: update ERP system, buy billing system, develop new CRM capability
  - User support: develop email system, develop user training
- A program manager provides leadership and direction for the project managers heading the projects within the program

## Slide 32: Organizational Project Management (1 of 2)

- Organizational project management is "a framework in which portfolio, program, and project management are integrated with organizational enablers in order to achieve strategic objectives" (PMBOK® Guide — Sixth Edition, 2017)
- Example:
  - One large portfolio for the organization
  - The portfolio is divided into categories: marketing, materials, IT, HR
  - IT portfolio category is divided into categories
    - Venture transforms the business, e.g. IT project provides kiosks in stores and Internet to quickly obtain customer feedback and develop closer partnerships with customer
    - Growth increases revenues, e.g. IT project provides info on website in foreign languages to grow business in those countries
    - Core runs the business, e.g. IT projects provides computers for new employees

## Slide 36: Who is this?

- Someone who owns the project and provides resources and support for the project, program, or portfolio in order to enable its success.
- Someone who is responsible for planning, organizing, and directing the completion of a specific project while ensuring the project is on time, on budget, and within scope.
- Someone who can be thought of as a "super" project manager and is responsible for planning and overseeing the successful delivery of the program's output/product.
- Someone who is responsible for managing and leveraging the life cycle of investments, initiatives, programs, projects, and outcomes to optimally achieve enterprise goals and objectives.
- Someone who is impacted by the outcome of a project, has an interest in the success of the project, and can have a positive or negative influence on the project.

- 1990s Companies began creating PMOs

## Slide 42: Ethics in Project Management

- PMI's Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct applies to PMI members and those who hold or in the process of PMI certification
  - Several questions on the PMP® exam are related to professional responsibility, including ethics
- PMI's Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct addresses responsibility, respect, fairness, and honesty

## Slide 43: Project Management Software

- There are hundreds of different products to assist in performing project management
- Three main categories of tools:
  - Low-end tools: Handle single or smaller projects well, cost under $200 per user, e.g. Basecamp, Smartsheet, Zoho
  - Midrange tools: Handle multiple projects and users, cost $200-$1,000 per user, Microsoft Project Professional is still the most popular
  - High-end tools: Also called enterprise project management software, often licensed on a per-user basis, e.g. Microsoft Project Portfolio Management (PPM), Oracle, ServiceNow, Autodesk, SAP, Odoo
- Several free or open-source tools are also available, e.g. ProjectLibre, LibrePlan, OpenProject, GanttProject
- Several tools focus on managing agile projects, e.g. Asana, Trello, Jira, Wrike, VersionOne, Monday

## Slide 47: Chapter Summary

- A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result
- Project management is the application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to project activities to meet project requirements
- A program is a group of related projects managed in a coordinated way
- Project portfolio management involves organizing and managing projects and programs as a portfolio of investments
- Project managers play a key role in helping projects and organizations succeed
- The project management profession continues to grow and mature
